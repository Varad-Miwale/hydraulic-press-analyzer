Best Circuit – Detailed Performance

Target vs simulated

The brief defines no tolerance bands, so none are invented; status labels only compare the simulated value with the target.

Position follows the four phases: fast approach, slow pressing, hold, then return. Stroke depth and the slope in each phase show whether the cycle matches the brief.
Speed is negative when the cylinder moves down. Dashed lines are the brief's target speeds. The oscillations at phase changes come from the abrupt valve commands in the model.
Pressure rises during the working phase because the cylinder must generate the pressing force. Peak head/rod pressures indicate the load and circuit behaviour.
Fast motion needs far more flow than slow working for a given cylinder area. Supplying full pump flow during working can create bypass/relief losses.

Hydraulic power: P = p × Q  ·  Hydraulic energy: E = ∫ p(t) × Q(t) dt. This is hydraulic energy at the pump; motor/electrical efficiency, the 0.9 pump efficiency and the 10 bar line loss are not added.

Data Science – Anomaly Detection

Proof of concept – simulated data

Workflow: normal simulation → reference signal → synthetic abnormality + noise → residual calculation → threshold detection. The method is a statistical residual-based anomaly detector, not trained machine learning, and does not claim predictive maintenance or failure prediction. The reference is the same simulation the fault is added to, so detection rates are not measured here.

Hopsan Simulation Evidence

Hopsan simulation recordings used to generate the result data analysed in this dashboard. All three circuits share the same cylinder, load and duty cycle — only the hydraulic circuit topology differs.

Circuit 1 – Regenerative Counterbalancing

Regenerative circuit with counterbalance valve. Extends the cylinder rod-side oil back to the pump inlet during fast-down, reducing pump demand.

Circuit 2 – High-Low Pump

Two-pump (high-low) system. Large low-pressure pump provides fast traverse flow; small high-pressure pump takes over during working stroke. Achieves the greatest energy saving (~79% vs Circuit 1).

Circuit 3 – Transverse and Feed + Meter Out

Meter-out speed control with transverse feed valve. Simplest topology; speed is controlled by throttling the return-side flow. Energy consumption is similar to Circuit 1.
